## Abstract 

Standard autoregressive (AR) models process high-level task instructions, state his-tory, and transient tokens within a single shared sequence of tokens. Consequently, they lack the architectural mechanisms needed to isolate macro-objectives from context noise. To overcome this single-channel limitation, we introduce Latent-Steered Autoregressive (LS-AR) , a dual-channel architecture that decouples continuous goal steering from discrete token decoding via FiLM conditioning. We evaluate a Static Goal Encoder (P0) for persistent macro-objective retention across long rollouts and a Dynamic State Tracker (Pt) for recurrent latent updates dur-ing generation. On long-horizon retrieval past context limits ( H=1024 , W =500 ), LS-AR (Static) achieves 100% target recall where parameter-matched baselines collapse ( 0% ), while increasing throughput by ∼ 35% and cutting peak VRAM by 52 .8% . In Blocksworld planning under forced perturbations ( k=1 ), LS-AR (Dynamic) sustains an 89 .0% completion rate vs. 71 .0% for the baseline, though zero-shot entity scaling ( N → N + 1 ) exposes single-vector capacity limits (0% ). Finally, dual-channel authority analysis shows that text goal dropout es-tablishes latent-dominant control, offering structural defence against text prompt injection while introducing a latent vector attack surface. Code is available at github.com/agupta1024/future-conditional-llms. 

## 1 Introduction 

While autoregressive language models excel at open-ended sequence completion, they face a funda-mental structural mismatch when applied to goal-directed planning. Because these models condition decoding exclusively on context window, they are forced to process macro-objectives within the exact same token stream used for local, step-by-step token generation. As sequence lengths expand during multi-step execution, this single-channel paradigm suffers from severe attention dilution and context eviction [Liu et al., 2024b, Xiao et al., 2024]: overarching task instructions are either pushed beyond the active context window or diluted across long attention spans. Consequently, models gradually lose sight of primary goals, prioritising immediate local predicates at the expense of overall plan coherence [Valmeekam et al., 2023b]. Existing mitigations, such as context window scaling [Chen et al., 2023, Child et al., 2019] or external memory architectures [Chhikara et al., 2025, Ho et al., 2025], incur substantial computational overhead and retrieval latency without resolving the underlying single-channel constraint. To overcome this structural bottleneck, we introduce the Latent-Steered Autoregressive (LS-AR) architecture, a framework that introduces a dedicated continuous channel to causal decoders to evaluate macro-objective retention over extended generation horizons. Inspired by Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) [Dawid and LeCun, 2024], we adopt a non-generative, latent-predictive objective to model macro-intent. Rather than predicting future trajectory steps in token space, LS-AR trains a goal predictor to forecast target state representations directly in latent space

By injecting these latent vectors into the decoder’s residual stream via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layers [Perez et al., 2018], we architecturally decouple continuous macro-objective steering from discrete token decoding. We evaluate two operational paradigms: a Static Goal Encoder (P0) designed to bypass context eviction across extended sequence horizons, and a 

Dynamic State Tracker (Pt) that recurrently updates the latent steering vector to absorb intermediate trajectory perturbations. 

Experimental scope. To isolate the architectural impacts of latent steering from confounding variables such as Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) fragmentation or opaque pre-training priors our empirical evaluation is strictly bounded to synthetic, domain-tokenised environments (procedural Treasure Hunt and interactive Blocksworld ). We benchmark LS-AR against parameter-matched autoregressive baselines across controlled 32M and 85M parameter scales. 

Summary of contributions. 

• Latent-Steered Autoregressive (LS-AR) framework: We present a JEPA-inspired architecture that routes latent vectors into autoregressive decoders via FiLM layers, establishing a fixed-dimensional alternative to temporal context scaling. • Context expiration mitigation ( P0): On procedural retrieval tasks, the Static variant main-tains 100% target recall over extended horizons ( H = 1024 , context window W = 500 ) where parameter-matched standard AR baselines collapse to 0% , while reducing peak VRAM by up to 

52 .8% and increasing throughput by ∼ 35% .• Dynamic resilience and error recovery ( Pt): In interactive Blocksworld environments, the Dy-namic variant continuously updates latent predictions to absorb trajectory perturbations, achieving an 89 .0% task completion rate compared to 71 .0% for the baseline under forced single-error recovery ( k = 1 ). • Capacity limits of latent steering: We demonstrate that scaling out-of-distribution entity counts (N → N + 1 ) causes complete task failure ( 0.0% success), exposing the fundamental capacity bounds of single-vector steering. • Security implications of channel dominance: Through goal-swapping and blank-goal ablations, we analyse the control hierarchy between textual and latent channels. We reveal a new attack surface: training curricula featuring textual goal dropout grant dominant authority to the steering channel, allowing latent vectors to covertly override explicit text instructions. 

## 2 Related work 

2.1 Context extensions and recurrent architectures 

Long-horizon degradation in autoregressive models is typically framed as a context capacity constraint [Mohtashami and Jaggi, 2023]. Mitigation strategies rely on scaling causal attention windows [Chen et al., 2023, Child et al., 2019, Liu et al., 2024a] or augmenting decoders with external memory and KV-cache eviction policies [Lewis et al., 2020, Xiao et al., 2024]. However, these approaches incur quadratic O(N 2) compute costs or rely on precise local retrieval queries without providing structural goal isolation. Selective State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba [Gu et al., 2021, Gu and Dao, 2023] avoid quadratic costs but suffer from signal dilution, mixing macro-instructions and microscopic syntax within a single recurrent state. In contrast, LS-AR decouples macro-objectives into a dedicated steering channel, preventing target intent from being overwritten during local token generation. 

2.2 Explicit planning and agentic reasoning 

Autoregressive planning on multi-step benchmarks [Valmeekam et al., 2023a] often fails due to causal myopia, repeatedly trapping models in local optima such as the Sussman Anomaly [Sussman, 1973]. Reasoning frameworks like Chain-of-Thought [Wei et al., 2022], Tree-of-Thoughts [Yao et al., 2023], and ReAct [Yao et al., 2022] force models to verbalise plans in token space. Over extended rollouts, these text scratchpads consume context window capacity and remain susceptible to context eviction and task amnesia. LS-AR removes macro-goal steering from the token stream entirely: target states are projected into continuous latent vectors, freeing textual generation to focus strictly on local step execution while preserving global goal alignment. 

2.3 Predictive architectures (JEPA) and latent conditioning 

Our methodology builds on Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) [Dawid and LeCun, 2024, Assran et al., 2023, 2025], which predict abstract representations rather than exact future tokens. While LLM-JEPA [Huang et al., 2025] restricts latent prediction to an auxiliary training objective, 2and Agentic-JEPA [Rodrigo-Ginés, 2026] isolates latent representations for offline state simulation, LS-AR actively integrates these predictions into the decoding loop. By injecting the predictions directly into the decoder’s residual stream via FiLM layers [Perez et al., 2018], LS-AR converts latent representations into dynamic steering vectors for autoregressive generation.

## 4 Experiments 

To evaluate latent steering independently of orthogonal prompting or retrieval mechanisms (e.g., Chain-of-Thought or RAG), we compare LS-AR directly against parameter-matched autoregressive causal decoders across strictly controlled parameter footprints, tokeniser vocabularies, and training corpora. 

4.1 Mitigating context expiration (Treasure Hunt) Experimental setup & baselines We benchmark LS-AR (Static) on fixed-target procedural retrieval using the Treasure Hunt dataset ( 50 k training trajectories, 500 evaluation instances; Ap-pendix B.1), which combines elements of Needle in a Haystack [Nelson et al., 2024] and Passkey retrieval [Mohtashami and Jaggi, 2023]. Using standard BPE tokenisation ( |V | = 50 , 257 ), the parameter-matched AR baseline employs a 20-layer causal decoder ( ∼ 89 M parameters). LS-AR (Static) matches this capacity ( ∼ 85 M parameters) using a 4-layer base encoder ( Ebase ), a 4-layer causal decoder ( Dbase ), weight-tied embeddings, and per-layer FiLM projection heads. In each test instance, a prompt specifying a target item (e.g., Ruby Amulet ) and a 4-digit passkey (e.g., 4892 ) is followed by an irrelevant distractor sequence of length H. We enforce prefix forcing – injecting distractor tokens before terminal retrieval – and set the context window W = 500 across expanding horizons H ∈ { 64 , 128 , 256 , 512 , 1024 }. When H > W , prompt tokens slide out of active causal attention, testing recall beyond the context boundary. The empirical results across our benchmarks demonstrate that LS-AR maintains semantic objective retention over extended generation horizons by leveraging continuous global conditioning. By analysing the context expiration failures of the AR baseline and the recovery mechanics of our framework, we identify broader implications for goal-directed sequence modelling. 

5.1 Latent steering vs. temporal context scaling 

Standard context window expansion ( W ) remains vulnerable to attention dilution and prompt eviction. By projecting macro-objectives into a persistent latent vector Pt, LS-AR isolates high-level goal steering from local token generation. This decoupling maintains global steering authority past active context boundaries while delivering ∼ 35% throughput gains by offloading compute to a shallow 4-layer decoder backbone. 

5.2 Limits of out-of-distribution structural generalisation 

Single-vector conditioning exhibits clear structural capacity bounds under out-of-distribution (OOD) shift ( N = 6 block scaling yields 0% success across all variants). A single fixed-dimensional latent vector lacks the relational capacity required for zero-shot combinatorial binding over unobserved object configurations. 

5.3 Modality authority and security dynamics 

Adversarial evaluations reveal that channel dominance – prioritising prompt text or latent vectors – is an engineered artifact of the training curriculum: • Latent-dominant regime (goal dropout): Textual goal dropout establishes latent vectors as the primary steering channel ( 67 .2% injected goal execution). This provides structural defence against text-based prompt injections [Greshake et al., 2023], but creates vulnerability to gradient-based vector perturbations [Zou et al., 2023, Qi et al., 2024]. • Text-dominant regime (standard training): Standard joint training grounds authority in prompt text ( 56 .2% primary goal execution), suppressing unsanctioned vector swaps ( 0.4% ) and ensuring auditability. Rather than enforcing a fixed trust model, LS-AR allows developers to tune modality authority based on deployment constraints – selecting latent dominance when processing untrusted text, or text dominance when auditability and latent robustness are paramount. 

5.4 Limitations 

LS-AR exhibits four primary operational boundaries: (1) evaluation is restricted to synthetic symbolic environments, leaving continuous language transfer for future work; (2) compressing macro-intent into a single vector causes minor sub-word BPE digit scrambling during numeric passkey extraction; (3) single-vector bottlenecking bounds combinatorial resolution on OOD tasks; and (4) comparisons against unaugmented AR models serve as a preliminary bounds check, necessitating future evaluation against memory-augmented baselines (e.g., continuous KV-caches) to isolate architectural gains. 

## 6 Conclusion and future work 

We introduced LS-AR , an architecture that modulates autoregressive token generation with latent goal vectors via FiLM conditioning. LS-AR (Static) achieves 100% semantic goal recall well beyond context limits ( H = 1024 , W = 500 ) with a +35% speedup over parameter-matched baselines, while LS-AR (Dynamic) yields 89 .0% recovery under off-trajectory perturbations. 

Future Directions. To address single-vector compression bounds, we outline three key extensions: (1) Multi-slot latent memory: expanding single vectors into k-slot matrices ( Pt ∈ Rk×d) to enable N → N + 1 combinatorial scaling; (2) Adaptive updating: replacing fixed step triggers with entropy-guided dynamic state updates; and (3) Open-vocabulary scaling: evaluating latent steering on multi-billion parameter backbones across complex agentic benchmarks (e.g., ALFWorld, WebArena).

## A Appendix A: Architectural rationale for dynamic state tracking 

A.1 Context aggregation: trigger-position vs. explicit pooling 

To construct the historical context summary si from generated sequence x1: i, we extract the hidden state at the trigger boundary: 

si = Ebase (x1: i)i ∈ Rd

We opted for this single-position summary over explicit mean- or max-pooling across the sub-action tokens for two main reasons: 10 • Implicit Causal Aggregation: Because Ebase employs causal self-attention, the token representa-tion at the terminal position i naturally attends to and integrates contextual information from all preceding tokens in the prefix x1: i.• Computational Efficiency: Utilising si directly avoids storing intermediate activation matri-ces across token spans for auxiliary pooling, reducing memory overhead during long-trajectory generation. 

A.2 Event-driven vs. fixed-interval triggers 

Instead of invoking the Dynamic State Tracker at fixed token intervals (e.g., every N tokens), updates are triggered strictly upon encountering domain-specific event boundary tokens (e.g., comma ‘,’ separators in BlocksWorld). This design choice is grounded in the structure of sequential decision-making tasks: • Semantic Alignment: Plan execution consists of variable-length sub-actions. Fixed-interval triggering risks updating the target latent prior Pi mid-action, forcing the model to re-steer before a sub-goal is completed. • Variable-Length Robustness: Event-driven triggers ensure that state updates naturally adapt to actions of varying token lengths without requiring artificial padding or manual segment alignment.

B.3 Implementation and reproducibility details B.3.1 Domain-specific tokenisation 

A known confounding factor when evaluating logical reasoning in language models is Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) fragmentation [Chai et al., 2024, Zhang et al., 2024]. Standard tokenisers frequently split single-character identifiers (e.g., blocks A through F) or fuse structural delimiters with adjacent action verbs, artificially complicating state tracking. To isolate architectural capacity from lexical artifacts, we adopt a clean word-level tokenisation scheme for the Blocksworld domain. We map environmental entities, action primitives ( pickup , putdown , stack , unstack ), and rela-tional predicates directly to individual vocabulary IDs. Key structural delimiters, including [INIT] ,12 [GOAL] , [DONE] , and the update trigger comma ( ,), are registered as immutable special tokens. This 1:1 mapping ensures that the latent steering vector Pi interacts with clean, unfragmented physical semantics.

B.3.3 Training hyperparameters: Hardware and computational budget All models were implemented in PyTorch. Experiments were conducted on an internal cluster using NVIDIA A100 (80GB VRAM) GPUs (utilising PyTorch Distributed Data Parallel for multi-GPU runs). Individual training runs required approximately 3–5 wall-clock hours for 32M parameter models and 5–8 wall-clock hours for 85M parameter models. Accounting for preliminary hyperparameter tuning, loss stability testing, and exploratory/failed iterations, the total computational budget for the entire project is estimated at approximately 350 A100 GPU hours. Models were trained using the AdamW optimizer ( β1 = 0 .9, β 2 = 0 .999 , weight decay = 1 × 10 −4)with a global gradient clipping norm of 1.0. The detailed training schedules across experimental runs are as follows: • Phase 1 (Base Domain Adaptation): The base model ( d = 512 , L = 4 layers) is fine-tuned for 30 epochs at a peak learning rate of 1 × 10 −4 with a 10% linear warmup and linear decay. • Phase 2 (Predictor Alignment): Epred is trained for 30 epochs at a peak learning rate of 2 × 10 −4

with batch size B = 32 , keeping base encoder Ebase frozen. • Phase 3 (Conditioned Generation): The causal decoder Dbase , zero-initialised FiLM projections (Wγ , W β , b γ , b β ), and the Dynamic State Tracker are trained end-to-end for 30 epochs at a uniform learning rate of 1 × 10 −4.
