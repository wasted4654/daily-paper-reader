ABSTRACT
Generative modeling has shown strong promise for multivariate time mseries
(MTS) forecasting, especially scale to high-dimensional settings. Diffusion-based
methods achieve competitive performance but typically require many sampling
steps at inference. VAE-based non-iterative forecasting frameworks have there-
fore emerged as an efficient alternative. Within this line of work, vector quan-
tization (VQ) enables controllable latent space modeling by mapping multivari-
ate series into compact discrete representations. Existing VQ-based forecasting
methods, however, typically rely on autoregressive (AR) token generation, which
suffers from exposure bias and training-inference mismatch. Flow matching pro-
vides an efficient non-autoregressive alternative for latent forecasting, but existing
formulations usually initialize transport from a generic Gaussian prior. We instead
observe that the trained VQ codebook already captures representative latent pro-
totypes and can thus serve as a more informative prior for flow matching. Based
on this insight, we propose ProtoFlow, a forecasting framework that combines
vector-quantized autoencoding with Prototype-prior Flow matching. Our method
first maps multivariate sequences into a discrete latent space, then constructs a
structured prior from the learned codebook, and finally learns a DiT-based rec-
tified flow to transport samples from this prior to future latent representations
conditioned on historical observations. By replacing generic noise initialization
with a learned prototype prior, ProtoFlow avoids the rollout mismatch of AR to-
ken prediction and promotes faster training convergence. Extensive experiments
on benchmark datasets show that it consistently achieves superior forecasting per-
formance with efficient inference.

INTRODUCTION
Multivariate time series (MTS) forecasting is an important research problem with broad applica-
tions in domains such as economics Huang et al. (2024), traffic systems Shao et al. (2022); Jiang
et al. (2023), energy management Chen et al. (2024), and weather prediction Wu et al. (2020; 2023).
In many real-world scenarios, forecasting involves a large number of variables evolving over time,
making high-dimensional MTS forecasting particularly challenging. As a result, developing effec-
tive forecasting models for high-dimensional multivariate sequences has become an increasingly
important topic.
Generative models provide a natural and effective framework for this setting, and have shown strong
potential for scaling to high-dimensional MTS forecasting Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015); Dinh et al.
(2016); Kingma et al. (2019); Song et al. (2020). Among them, diffusion-based methods have
achieved competitive performance in recent years Rasul et al. (2021); Shen & Kwok (2023); Meijer
& Chen (2024); Cao et al. (2024); Wang et al. (2024); Ruan et al. (2025). However, their inference
typically requires many denoising steps, leading to substantial computational cost.
This limitation has motivated increasing interest in VAEs-based non-iterative forecasting frame-
works, which perform generation in a learned latent space. Within this family, vector quantization
(VQ) provides a controllable latent space modeling strategy by mapping complex multivariate se-
quences into a compact discrete codebook. Such vector-quantized representations have recently

shown promise in token-based time series modeling and forecasting Talukder et al. (2024); Chen
et al. (2024); Feng et al. (2025). However, existing VQ-based forecasting methods typically rely on
autoregressive (AR) token generation. Since AR models are usually trained with teacher forcing,
they depend on ground-truth previous tokens during training but on their own predictions during
inference, resulting in train–test mismatch and error accumulation during rollout Schmidt (2019);
Bengio et al. (2015).
Flow matching (FM) learns continuous transport and can support non-autoregressive generation Liu
et al. (2022). Recent time series applications include TSFlow, which introduces Gaussian-process
priors Kollovieh et al. (2024); TFM, which learns history-conditioned Neural SDEs through
simulation-free training Zhang et al. (2024); and FlowTime, which adopts teacher-forced autore-
gressive forecasting El-Gazzar & van Gerven (2025). These methods primarily model transport
in observation space, where high-dimensional MTS forecasting requires capturing complex tempo-
ral and cross-variable dependencies in the joint predictive distribution. ProtoFlow addresses this
challenge by jointly generating future sequences in a compact vector-quantized latent space. Its pro-
totype prior incorporates learned latent centers, empirical frequencies, and local variability into the
source distribution, providing a structured initialization for conditional transport. Empirically, this
initialization supports earlier FM training stabilization than its Gaussian-prior counterpart, while
enabling accurate few-step forecasting in high-dimensional MTS settings.
Based on this observation, we propose ProtoFlow, a prototype-prior flow matching framework for
high-dimensional multivariate time series forecasting. ProtoFlow combines vector-quantized repre-
sentations with a structured source prior derived from learned codebook prototypes and their empir-
ical statistics. It jointly generates future latent sequences through non-autoregressive flow matching,
avoiding the ground-truth versus generated-token conditioning mismatch associated with teacher-
forced autoregressive rollout.
Experiments on five real-world benchmarks with several predic-
tion horizons demonstrate strong overall performance against competitive diffusion- and flow-based
baselines with few ODE sampling steps. Further analyses show earlier FM training stabilization and
reduced sampling-step sensitivity relative to Gaussian initialization, supporting the effectiveness of
reusing learned latent structure for transport initialization.
Main Contributions.
• We propose ProtoFlow, a framework for high-dimensional multivariate time series
forecasting that combines vector-quantized representations with non-autoregressive flow
matching to jointly generate future latent sequences, avoiding the conditioning mismatch
associated with teacher-forced autoregressive rollout.
• We develop a structured source prior from learned prototype centers and empirical fre-
quencies, extending the codebook’s role from latent quantization to transport initialization.
Compared with Gaussian initialization, this design empirically supports earlier FM training
stabilization and reduced sensitivity to the sampling-step budget.
• Experiments on five real-world benchmarks with testing prediction horizons demonstrate
strong overall probabilistic and deterministic forecasting performance against competitive
diffusion- and flow-based baselines with substantially fewer sampling steps.

METHOD
In this section, we present ProtoFlow, a prototype-prior flow-matching framework for multivariate
time series forecasting. ProtoFlow first learns a vector-quantized tokenizer to map future sequences
into a compact discrete latent space, and then performs conditional rectified flow matching initialized
from a learned codebook prior. Thus, the codebook serves not only as a latent discretizer, but also
as a structured prior for latent generation.

EXPERIMENTS
Benchmarks. Our evaluation uses five widely adopted real-world benchmarks for high-dimensional
MTS forecasting: Solar and Electricity Lai et al. (2018), Traffic and Taxi Salinas et al. (2019),
and Wikipedia Gasthaus et al. (2019). These datasets span energy, transportation, and web-traffic
applications, with sampling intervals ranging from 30 minutes to one day. Additional dataset details
are provided in Appendix C.1.
Baselines. We compare ProtoFlow with strong generative forecasting baselines from the diffu-
sion and flow families. For diffusion-based forecasting, we include TimeGrad Rasul et al. (2021),
CSDI Tashiro et al. (2021), TSDiff Kollovieh et al. (2023), MG-TSD Fan et al. (2024), DyDiff Guo
et al. (2025), and NsDiff Ye et al. (2025). We additionally consider the recent popular flow-based
approachs FlowTime El-Gazzar & van Gerven (2025) and TSFlow Kollovieh et al. (2025). Detailed
baseline descriptions are deferred to Appendix B.
Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate both probabilistic and deterministic forecasting quality. For prob-
abilistic forecasting, we report the continuous ranked probability score on the aggregated series,
CRPSsum Matheson & Winkler (1976). For deterministic accuracy, we report NRMSEsum, which
provides a scale-normalized comparison across datasets with different value ranges. Following stan-
dard probabilistic forecasting protocols, all results are computed from 100 generated samples.More
details on the evaluation metrics are given in Appendix C.2.
Implementation Details. We use a history length of 96 and prediction lengths of {48, 96}, fol-
lowing high-dimensional time series forecasting settings Rasul et al. (2021); Tashiro et al. (2021).
RevIN Kim et al. (2021) is applied except Solar. All experiments run on a single NVIDIA A100
GPU with 40 GB memory, with results averaged over three runs. More details are shown in Ap-
pendix Tables 6 and 7.

EFFECTS OF LATENT REPRESENTATION AND GENERATION STRATEGY.
We analyze the effects of latent representation and generation strategy in Table 2.
1) VQ-based variants outperform their VAE-based counterparts on both metrics across all five
datasets under both AR and FM generation. Specifically, replacing VAE with VQ reduces the
dataset-averaged CRPSsum and NRMSEsum by 75.0% and 73.3% under AR generation, and by 57.3%
and 51.9% under Gaussian-prior FM, respectively. These consistent improvements support the ef-
fectiveness of vector-quantized representations across different generation strategies. 2) With VQ
representations, ProtoFlow-G consistently outperforms VQ + AR across all five datasets, reducing
the dataset-averaged CRPSsum and NRMSEsum by 19.4% and 14.2%, respectively. By modeling
the future latent sequence jointly, non-autoregressive FM avoids the discrepancy between condi-
tioning on ground-truth preceding tokens during teacher-forced training and on generated tokens
during AR inference. These results support the advantage of non-autoregressive FM in this set-
ting. 3) Overall, these results support the combination of vector-quantized representations and non-
autoregressive flow matching. ProtoFlow further improves upon ProtoFlow-G on both metrics across
all five datasets. Together with the earlier stabilization of its normalized training loss in Figure 2(a),
these results support the benefit of incorporating learned prototype information into the FM source
distribution.

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose ProtoFlow, a prototype-prior flow matching framework that integrates
vector-quantized representations with non-autoregressive transport for high-dimensional multivari-
ate time series forecasting. By constructing the source distribution from learned prototypes and their
local statistics, ProtoFlow achieves strong forecasting performance with few ODE steps, alongside
earlier stabilization of flow matching training and reduced sensitivity to the sampling budget. Be-
yond these empirical gains, our findings highlight a broader role for learned codebooks: their struc-
ture can inform both the representation of time series and the initialization of generative transport.
This perspective motivates closer integration of representation learning and source-prior design as a
promising direction for efficient generative time series forecasting.

B
RELATED WORK
We discuss two lines of research underlying ProtoFlow: vector-quantized latent modeling and dif-
fusion/flow matching for multivariate time series forecasting.
Vector-quantized Latent Modeling. Vector quantization Van Den Oord et al. (2017) maps con-
tinuous representations to a finite set of learned embeddings, enabling discrete latent modeling. Its
promising performance in time series forecasting and generation has motivated growing interest in
this representation strategy. For example, in time series forecasting task, VQ-TR Rasul et al. (2024)
introduces quantization within attention for computationally efficient forecasting and HDT Feng
et al. (2025) formulates multivariate forecasting through hierarchical discrete token modeling. Re-
lated generation methods explore diverse latent structures and generation strategies within VQ-based
time series modeling. TimeVQVAE Lee et al. (2023) employs bidirectional prior modeling, while
SDformer Chen et al. (2024) combines similarity-driven vector quantization with autoregressive or
masked token modeling. MSDformer Feng et al. (2026) extends discrete modeling through multi-
scale tokenization and autoregressive generation. SDFlow Li et al. (2026) instead performs non-
autoregressive generation through variational flow matching in a frozen VQ latent space, incorpo-
rating categorical supervision over codebook indices. In addition, TOTEM Talukder et al. (2024)
learns a shared vector-quantized tokenizer across diverse domains, enabling transferable discrete
representations for forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection tasks. ProtoFlow additionally
uses the codebook as a source of distributional information, constructing a prototype prior for con-
tinuous transport in the latent embedding space.
Diffusion and Flow Matching for Forecasting.
Diffusion-based methods have demonstrated
strong performance in time series analysis, particularly in multivariate forecasting, where iterative
denoising enables flexible modeling of future uncertainty. TimeGrad Rasul et al. (2021) combines
diffusion with autoregressive prediction, while CSDI Tashiro et al. (2021) and TimeDiff Shen &
Kwok (2023) investigate conditional reconstruction and non-autoregressive prediction, respectively.
14


Preprint
TSDiff Kollovieh et al. (2023) enables forecasting with an unconditional diffusion model through
inference-time guidance. Subsequent methods incorporate multi-granularity supervision MG-TSD
Fan et al. (2024), temporal dynamics DyDiff Guo et al. (2025), and time-varying uncertainty NsDiff
Ye et al. (2025). Although effective, iterative sampling can impose substantial inference costs.
Flow matching learns transport from a source distribution to the data distribution through vector-
field regression, enabling ODE-based generation that can support accurate sampling with fewer
integration steps through suitable probability-path design Lipman et al. (2022); Tong et al. (2023).
For time series forecasting task, FlowTime El-Gazzar & van Gerven (2025) develops autoregres-
sive flow matching in observation space, whereas TSFlow Kollovieh et al. (2025) incorporates
Gaussian-process priors to encode temporal structure and supports both conditional and uncon-
ditional modeling. These approaches motivate studying both transport dynamics and source dis-
tributions. ProtoFlow explores this direction by constructing a source prior from learned latent
prototypes, supporting accurate forecasting with few ODE sampling steps while facilitating earlier
stabilization of flow matching training.
Positioning of ProtoFlow. ProtoFlow connects vector-quantized representations with prototype-
prior flow matching. Its codebook serves two roles: representing target sequences and defining a
structured source distribution through prototype centers, empirical frequencies, and local variabil-
ity. Conditioned on historical context, a non-autoregressive vector field jointly transports the future
latent sequence, which is then decoded into forecasts. The distinguishing design is the reuse
of learned representation structure to initialize latent transport, providing a data-informed
source prior that supports earlier FM training stabilization and accurate forecasting with few
sampling steps. Our experiments examine how this initialization affects FM training dynamics,
few-step forecasting, and sensitivity to the sampling budget.

Following standard practice, we adopt a strictly chronological split based on a fixed cutoff time,
partitioning each dataset into 60% training, 20% validation, and 20% test. We further evaluate with
a rolling-window protocol on the test segment: forecasts are initiated from multiple start times that
are evenly spaced after the last training timestamp, and metrics are aggregated across windows. For
hourly datasets, we use 7 rolling windows; for the remaining datasets we use 5 windows, except for
Taxi where 57 windows are employed to cover the full test period.

LIMITATIONS
ProtoFlow constructs its prototype prior from global training statistics and keeps it fixed during fore-
casting. Although the learned transport is conditioned on historical observations, the source prior
does not adapt its probability allocation to individual forecasting contexts. A promising direction
is to develop context-dependent prototype weighting, allowing initialization to reflect the current
temporal regime while retaining the efficiency of few-step generation. We leave this direction for
our future work.
