## 摘要
现有的基于训练的语音情感编辑方法通常需要大量特定任务的训练，且过程可能不稳定。这促使我们研究大规模文本转语音（TTS）模型的预训练生成动力学是否可以直接被操纵，以实现无需训练的情感编辑。为了回答这个问题，我们通过构建受控测试集并系统地诊断生成轨迹上的编辑效果，探测了预训练流匹配（flow-matching）和混合TTS模型的可编辑性。我们的分析表明，预训练TTS模型在情感方面具有显著的可编辑性，但这种可编辑性依赖于架构和轨迹，且可能被早期的流匹配步骤所破坏，同时跨说话人的情感迁移会携带除情感之外的其他声学属性。为了解决这些局限性，我们提出了SEmoEdit，这是首个无需训练的框架，它将情感编辑表述为源情感与目标情感之间的动态速度传输，从而在预训练TTS模型内直接实现稳健的、基于流的语音情感编辑。SEmoEdit统一了三种核心操作：情感替换、情感擦除和连续情感插值，无需参数更新或特定任务的优化。为了系统地评估这些能力，我们引入了SEmoEditBench，这是一个包含600个编辑案例的数据集，并在最先进（SOTA）的模型和骨干网络上进行了广泛的实验。结果表明，SEmoEdit非常有效且适用范围广，优于现有的基于训练和激活引导的方法。最终，这项工作揭示了预训练语音流拥有丰富且潜在的情感编辑能力，为实际应用提供了有益的指导。代码、基准测试和音频样本可在 https://github.com/imxtx/SEmoEdit 获取。

## Abstract
Existing training-based speech emotion editing methods often require substantial task-specific training and can be unstable. This motivates us to investigate whether the pretrained generative dynamics of large-scale text-to-speech (TTS) models can be directly manipulated for training-free emotion editing. To answer this question, we probe the editability of pretrained flow-matching and hybrid TTS models by constructing a controlled test set and systematically diagnosing editing effects along the generative trajectory. Our analysis reveals that pretrained TTS models are substantially editable in emotion, but such editability is architecture- and trajectory-dependent and can be disrupted by early flow-matching steps, while cross-speaker emotion transport carries additional acoustic attributes beyond emotion. To address these limitations, we propose SEmoEdit, the first training-free framework that formulates emotion editing as dynamic velocity transport between source and target emotions, enabling robust, flow-based speech emotion editing directly within pretrained TTS models. SEmoEdit unifies three core operations: emotion replacement, emotion erasure, and continuous emotion interpolation, requiring neither parameter updates nor task-specific optimization. To systematically evaluate these capabilities, we introduce SEmoEditBench, a dataset comprising 600 editing cases, and conduct extensive experiments across state-of-the-art (SOTA) models and backbones. Our results show that SEmoEdit is highly effective and broadly applicable, outperforming existing training-based and activation-steering methods. Ultimately, this work reveals that pretrained speech flows possess rich, latent emotion-editing capabilities, providing useful guidance for real applications. Code, benchmark, and Audio samples are available at https://github.com/imxtx/SEmoEdit.
